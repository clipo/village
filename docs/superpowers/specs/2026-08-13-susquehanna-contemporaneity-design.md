# Bayesian Contemporaneity of Late Prehistoric Occupations in the Upper Susquehanna Valley

**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Status:** Design, approved for planning
**Data:** `data/Local Dates.xlsx` (167 radiocarbon determinations)

## 1. Research questions

1. How many settlements in this part of the Susquehanna valley were occupied at the same time, and how does that number change through the sequence?
2. How long was each occupation, and how much do occupations overlap?
3. How strongly do the data support contemporaneous occupation as against strictly serial occupation?

The analysis must answer these as posterior distributions with stated uncertainty, not as point estimates.

## 2. Data

### 2.1 Contents

The spreadsheet holds 167 determinations in a single sheet with columns `site`, `material`, `lab_no`, `c14_age`, `c14_error`, `reference`, and two unnamed note columns. Radiocarbon ages range from 200 to 1760 BP. Errors range from 20 to 210 years.

### 2.2 Site and context parsing

The `site` column concatenates site name and excavation context (`Bates_66`, `Chenango_Point_F102`, `Owego Southside Sewage Treatment_Sq 7/70`). The site is the analytical unit, because the research question concerns villages. Context is retained so that inferred occupation phases can be checked against excavation units.

Parsing is not mechanical, so it is implemented as a **checked-in lookup table** (`data/site_parse.csv`) mapping every distinct `site` string to a site and a context, generated by `R/build_lookup_tables.R` and then audited. The table is the authority, not the rule that produced it.

Four labels are merged into other sites:

| Label | Merged into | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| `Broome_Tech_PAF` | `Broome_Tech` | AA-31005, AA-31006 and AA-31007 are consecutive lab numbers spanning both labels, and `Broome_Tech_1` cites Miroff_Kudrle_2017 like every PAF date. PAF is the Public Archaeology Facility. |
| `Chenango_Point_South` | `Chenango_Point` | Beta-35557 and Beta-35558 are consecutive and both trace to Wurst and Versaggi 1993. Directed by C. Lipo, 2026-08-13. |
| `Sidney Hangar` | `Sidney Airport` | Three loci of one airport complex investigated under separate projects. Directed by C. Lipo, 2026-08-13. |
| `Sidney AWOS` | `Sidney Airport` | As above. |

`Herrick Hollow I`, `II` and `V` remain separate, as do `Chenango_Shores_East`, `Owego River Crossing` and `Owego Southside Sewage Treatment`.

This yields **34 sites**. Nine carry six or more determinations (Thomas_Luckey 20, Chenango_Point 19, Bates 14, Roundtop 11, Broome_Tech 10, Jamba 8, Street 8, CCE_Site 7, Owego Southside Sewage Treatment 6), thirteen carry three to five, and twelve carry one or two.

### 2.3 Material classification

Three classes, in a checked-in lookup table (`data/material_classes.csv`) keyed on the verbatim material string:

- **short_lived** (n = 78): maize, bean, squash, seed, nutshell and named shells, marshelder. No meaningful inbuilt age. Mean error 40 years.
- **wood** (n = 68): wood, charred wood, wood charcoal, oak wood charcoal, "probably wood". May carry substantial inbuilt age. Mean error 75 years, maximum 210.
- **indeterminate** (n = 21): "charred material" (12), "charcoal" (3), "carbonized material" (1), and the mixed descriptions "carbonized wood, nutshell, corn" (4) and "wood/nushell" (1).

The indeterminate class is not assigned to wood by fiat. It carries **its own estimated inbuilt-age distribution**, separate from wood's, so the data determine how much inbuilt age those samples actually show. This removes the arbitrary call that a two-class scheme would force.

### 2.4 Sample-level preprocessing

Three determinations are altered before modeling. Each is recorded in the data-quality report with its justification.

**Failed replicate pair.** UGAMS-53046 (546 ± 20) and UGAMS-53046r (637 ± 25) at Bates_66 are the same sample measured twice, so they share one calendar date. A Ward and Wilson (1978) test gives T = 8.08 on 1 df, p = 0.0045, so they are not statistically consistent and cannot be pooled as they stand. They are combined into a single determination with the error multiplied by √T, giving **582 ± 44**. Entering both would double-count one sample and would let the model read a within-feature spread that cannot be real.

**Wiggle-matched pairs.** Four samples carry ring-year counts in the material field, from the tree-ring sequences reported in Birch, Manning, Hart and Lorentzen 2024:

| Site | Lab no. | Rings | ¹⁴C age | Offset within pair |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bates_46 | UGAMS-59365 | RY1–5 | 870 ± 25 | reference |
| Bates_46 | UGAMS-59366 | RY18–22 | 820 ± 25 | 17 years younger |
| Bates_89 | UGAMS-19367 | RY1–5 | 890 ± 20 | reference |
| Bates_89 | UGAMS-19368 | RY26–30 | 910 ± 20 | 25 years younger |

The calendar offset within each pair is known from ring counting, so the two members are not independent. Each pair becomes **one composite determination** whose likelihood is the product of the two member likelihoods with the known offset applied, `L_pair(t) = L_1(t) * L_2(t - delta)`, precomputed on the calendar grid exactly like every other likelihood variant of section 3.4. The ring counts also establish that these samples had at least 22 and 30 rings, which is direct evidence bearing on the wood inbuilt-age estimate.

After preprocessing, **164 determinations** enter the model from 167 spreadsheet rows.

## 3. Model

### 3.1 Notation

Index determinations by `i = 1..N`, sites by `s = 1..S`. Determination `i` belongs to site `s(i)` and material class `c(i)`. Observed age `y_i`, error `sigma_i`.

Calendar time is in cal BP throughout, so larger values are older. A phase runs from `a` (start, older) to `b` (end, younger) with `a > b`.

### 3.2 Occupation phases

Site `s` has `J_s` candidate occupation phases:

- `J_s = 4` for sites with 6 or more determinations (9 sites)
- `J_s = 2` for sites with 3 to 5 determinations (13 sites)
- `J_s = 1` for sites with 1 or 2 determinations (12 sites)

This gives 74 candidate phases across 34 sites, of which the model is expected to leave many inactive. The 12 single-phase sites contribute occupation windows whose duration is driven almost entirely by the hierarchical prior of section 3.7, and every result is reported both including and excluding them.

Each phase has a midpoint `m_sj` and a duration `d_sj > 0`, with `a_sj = m_sj + d_sj/2` and `b_sj = m_sj - d_sj/2`. Within a site the midpoints are declared `ordered`, which resolves label switching. Determinations within a phase are uniformly distributed across it, which is the standard Bronk Ramsey phase model.

Phase membership is unknown. Site `s` carries a simplex `pi_s` over its phases, with

```
pi_s ~ Dirichlet(alpha / J_s)
```

and `alpha = 1`. This is an overfitted mixture in the sense of Rousseau and Mengersen (2011): because `alpha / J_s < 1`, and each component carries two parameters, components not supported by data are driven toward zero weight rather than splitting the data arbitrarily. The number of occupation episodes at a site is therefore inferred rather than fixed.

### 3.3 Marginalizing the latent calendar dates

Stan cannot sample discrete parameters, so phase membership must be marginalized. Marginalizing over phases requires the probability of a determination given a phase, integrated over the unknown calendar date. That integral is computed analytically from precomputed quantities rather than by introducing latent date parameters.

For each determination, the calibration likelihood on a calendar grid is

```
L_i(t) = Normal(y_i | mu(t), sqrt(sigma_i^2 + tau(t)^2))
```

where `mu` and `tau` are the IntCal20 mean and error. This is evaluated on a uniform grid spanning 0 to 2500 cal BP at 1-year spacing and treated as piecewise linear between knots. Its exact integral

```
Phi_i(t) = integral from t_min to t of L_i(u) du
```

is then piecewise quadratic, and is evaluated in Stan at arbitrary `t` by locating the cell (constant time, since the grid is uniform) and applying

```
Phi(t) = Phi_g + L_g * h + (L_{g+1} - L_g) * h^2 / (2 * Delta),   h = t - t_g
```

This is continuously differentiable in `t`, so the gradient is well behaved for Hamiltonian Monte Carlo.

The probability of determination `i` given phase `(a, b)`, with the calendar date integrated out under the uniform-phase assumption, is

```
A_i(a, b) = (Phi_i(a) - Phi_i(b)) / (a - b)
```

Three consequences follow. The `1/(a-b)` factor is applied once per determination, which is the correct uniform-phase normalization. All `N` latent calendar-date parameters are eliminated, which removes the funnel geometry and speeds sampling. The likelihood is smooth in the phase boundaries.

### 3.4 Old wood

A wood sample's growth date precedes the depositional event by an inbuilt age. Modeling the inbuilt age as exponential with mean `kappa`, the event-date likelihood is the convolution of `L_i` with that exponential. Convolution is a fixed linear operation on the grid, so it is precomputed rather than evaluated during sampling.

The convolved likelihood and its integral are precomputed at six values of `kappa`: 0, 15, 30, 60, 120, and 240 years. A simplex `q^c` over these six values is estimated for each material class. `kappa = 0` recovers the uncorrected likelihood, so the model can conclude that inbuilt age is negligible.

Two simplexes are estimated, `q^wood` and `q^indeterminate`. Short-lived determinations are fixed at `kappa = 0`, that is `q^short_lived = (1, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0)`.

Each offset is identified by sites carrying both that class and short-lived material, since the model learns the offset from the within-site contrast rather than from an assumption imposed on it. The evidence available is thin and unevenly distributed:

| Site | short_lived | wood | indeterminate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Chenango_Point | 13 | 0 | 6 |
| Thomas_Luckey | 14 | 2 | 4 |
| Roundtop | 6 | 5 | 0 |
| Bates | 4 | 10 | 0 |
| Broome_Tech | 9 | 0 | 1 |
| CCE_Site | 6 | 1 | 0 |
| Deposit_Airport1 | 2 | 0 | 2 |
| Herrick Hollow II | 2 | 1 | 0 |
| Lamb | 1 | 0 | 2 |

The **wood** offset rests effectively on Roundtop and Bates, the only two sites with four or more of each class. This is weak, and it is weaker than it appeared under a two-class scheme, because moving "charcoal" and "charred material" out of wood removed Chenango_Point, Jamba, Deposit_Airport1 and Lamb from the sites that identify it. The ring-counted samples of section 2.4 supply a small amount of additional direct evidence.

The **indeterminate** offset is, unexpectedly, the better identified of the two, resting on Chenango_Point (13 against 6) and Thomas_Luckey (14 against 4).

Figure 7 reports both posteriors against their priors. Where a posterior tracks its prior, the correction is an assumption rather than an inference and will be described that way in the results. This is a real limitation of the dataset and no modeling choice removes it.

Prior: `q^wood` and `q^indeterminate` each `~ Dirichlet(1)`.

### 3.5 Outliers

Every determination carries a Christen (1994) outlier component: with probability `rho_c` the determination is drawn from a likelihood computed with error inflated by a factor of 5. Outlier rates `rho_c` are estimated separately for each of the three material classes, with `rho_c ~ Beta(2, 18)` (prior mean 0.10).

The inflated likelihood and its integral are precomputed alongside the others.

### 3.6 Full likelihood

For determination `i` at site `s`:

```
p(y_i) = sum over j of  pi_sj * [ (1 - rho_c) * sum over k of q^c_k * A_i^(kappa_k)(a_sj, b_sj)
                                + rho_c * A_i^(outlier)(a_sj, b_sj) ]
```

where `c = c(i)` is the material class, `q^short_lived` is fixed at `(1, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0)`, and `q^wood` and `q^indeterminate` are estimated. Evaluated on the log scale with `log_sum_exp`.

The two wiggle-matched composites of section 2.4 enter this expression unchanged. Their `A_i` is built from the paired likelihood `L_pair`, so the known ring offset is carried through every `kappa` variant and through the outlier component.

### 3.7 Duration prior

Duration is the parameter most exposed to prior choice, because a site with two or three determinations cannot constrain its own occupation length. Durations are therefore given a hierarchical prior pooled across all phases at all sites:

```
d_sj ~ Lognormal(mu_d, sigma_d)
mu_d ~ Normal(log 60, 1)
sigma_d ~ HalfNormal(1)
```

Pooling means that sites with enough determinations to constrain duration inform sites that do not.

**`[CITE-CHECK: village occupation duration]`** The prior mean of 60 years is a weakly informative placeholder. No source in this repository establishes a value for northern Iroquoian or Susquehanna village occupation length, and none is asserted here. The flag must be resolved, and the prior justified from a read source with page numbers, before any of this appears in manuscript prose.

Because this choice matters more than any other in the model, the primary model is refit under `mu_d ~ Normal(log 25, 1)` and `mu_d ~ Normal(log 150, 1)`, and every headline quantity is reported under all three. If the number of contemporaneous villages moves materially across them, that sensitivity is the finding and will be stated as such rather than resolved by picking one.

Phase midpoints receive a uniform prior over the calendar grid, subject to the within-site ordering.

## 4. Derived quantities

Computed in Stan's `generated quantities` block, so uncertainty propagates correctly.

**Phase allocation.** For each posterior draw, the discrete allocation `z_i` is drawn from its full conditional. This recovers the discrete structure that was marginalized out of the likelihood.

**Phase activity.** Phase `(s, j)` is active in a draw if at least one determination is allocated to it. This is the definition of an occupation episode used throughout: an episode exists when there is dated evidence for it. It requires no weight threshold.

**Site occupancy.** `O_s(t) = 1` if any active phase at site `s` covers calendar year `t`.

**Contemporaneous village count.** `C(t) = sum over s of O_s(t)`, evaluated on a 10-year reporting grid. This is the primary result. Its posterior is summarized by median and 50% and 95% credible intervals at each year. Reported twice: over all 34 sites, and over the 22 sites with three or more determinations, since the singly and doubly dated sites contribute windows whose width comes from the prior rather than from their own data.

**Pairwise overlap.** For each site pair, the number of calendar years in which both are occupied, `V_ss' = integral of O_s(t) * O_s'(t) dt`. Reported as a posterior distribution, together with `P(V >= 25 years)`. The existing repository code reports `P(V > 0)`, which saturates at 1 for any pair of fuzzy boundaries and carries no information; it is replaced, not extended.

**Occupation duration.** Posterior duration for each active phase, and total occupied span per site.

**Active phase count.** Posterior number of active phases per site, which reports directly on whether a site is a single occupation or a palimpsest.

**Log likelihood.** Per determination, for model comparison.

## 5. Model comparison

Four models, compared by leave-one-out cross-validation.

| Model | Structure | Question it represents |
|---|---|---|
| `M-single` | One phase covering all determinations | The valley was occupied as a single undifferentiated episode |
| `M-indep` | One phase per site, unconstrained | Each site is a single occupation, overlap unconstrained |
| `M-multi` | Multiple phases per site, unconstrained | Primary model |
| `M-seq` | One phase per site, all phases constrained non-overlapping | Villages were occupied strictly in series |

`M-single`, `M-indep`, and `M-multi` are the same Stan program under different data configurations, differing only in the site grouping and in `J_s`. `M-seq` is a separate program using a global ordered boundary vector.

`M-seq` requires an ordering of sites, and the space of orderings is combinatorially large. Sites are ordered by their median calibrated date, which is the ordering most favourable to the sequential hypothesis. This is stated explicitly as a limitation. If the sequential model still loses under its best available ordering, the conclusion against strict serial occupation is correspondingly stronger; if it wins, the result is suggestive rather than decisive, because the ordering was chosen using the data.

Comparison uses PSIS-LOO with Pareto-k diagnostics reported. Mixture models with few observations per component frequently produce unreliable Pareto-k values. Where more than five percent of k values exceed 0.7, exact 10-fold cross-validation is run instead and reported in place of PSIS-LOO.

## 6. Validation

No result is reported before these pass.

1. **Prior predictive check.** Simulate from the prior and confirm the implied distributions of occupation duration, phase count, and contemporaneous village count are archaeologically plausible and not pathological.
2. **Simulation recovery.** Simulate 30 datasets with known phase structure, using the real site sample sizes, the real measurement errors, and the real calibration curve. Fit `M-multi` and check coverage of credible intervals for duration, phase count, and `C(t)`. This is the test of whether multi-phase structure is recoverable at three determinations per site. The expectation is that it partly is not, and the recovered coverage determines which sites can carry an interpretation.
3. **Sampler diagnostics.** R-hat below 1.01, bulk and tail ESS above 400, zero divergent transitions, E-BFMI above 0.3. Failures are fixed, not reported around.
4. **Posterior predictive checks.** Compare observed determinations against the posterior predictive distribution, by site and by material class.
5. **Numerical check on the calibration machinery.** Compare the precomputed `Phi` interpolation against direct numerical integration of `L` for a sample of determinations and phase windows, and confirm agreement to within tolerance. Compare single-date calibrated distributions against `rcarbon::calibrate` output.
6. **Prior sensitivity.** All headline quantities under the three duration priors of section 3.7.

## 9. Expected outcome

Calibration curve structure sets a ceiling on resolution that no model can raise. Measuring IntCal20 directly over the study range, the curve does not form one long plateau. It has a set of discrete reversals, intervals where radiocarbon age falls as calendar age falls, within which calibration is multimodal and dating resolution collapses:

| Interval (cal BP) | Interval (AD) | Width |
|---|---|---|
| 411–344 | 1539–1606 | 67 yr |
| 886–837 | 1064–1113 | 49 yr |
| 613–577 | 1337–1373 | 36 yr |
| 1233–1199 | 717–751 | 34 yr |
| 1463–1429 | 487–521 | 34 yr |
| 1041–1014 | 909–936 | 27 yr |

Between these the curve retains good slope, so occupations falling in the intervening stretches should resolve to a few decades given enough short-lived determinations. This is a more favourable situation than a single broad plateau would present, and it means resolution will vary substantially from site to site depending on where an occupation happens to fall. The reporting must therefore be per-site rather than a single global statement about resolution.

The realistic expectation remains a broad posterior on the village count, wide durations at thinly dated sites, and a limited number of site pairs for which non-contemporaneity can be demonstrated outright. Figure 1 will be accompanied by the curve-reversal intervals marked, so that a narrowing or widening of the count credible interval can be read against calibration structure rather than mistaken for a change in settlement.

Demonstrating that contemporaneity cannot be resolved at a given scale is not the same as demonstrating that many villages were occupied simultaneously, and the write-up will not blur the two.

## 10. Defects in the existing code, and why it is not reused

Recorded so the decision is auditable.

- `stan/contemporaneous_model.stan` applies the uniform-phase normalization `-log(theta_start - theta_end)` once, where the uniform-phase model requires it once per determination. Combined with Stan's automatic Jacobian for the declared bounds on `calendar_dates`, the net penalty on phase duration is wrong, and durations are biased.
- `stan/partial_overlap_model.stan` maps `calendar_dates_raw` to `calendar_dates` through `inv_logit` and uses the result in the likelihood without the corresponding Jacobian adjustment. It also places a standard normal on the raw parameter, which implies a logit-normal distribution of dates within a phase, peaked at the centre, rather than the uniform distribution the phase model requires. Both bias duration.
- The `overlap_prob` generated quantity in the same program is a 0/1 indicator of any overlap. Averaged over draws it gives `P(overlap > 0)`, which saturates at 1 for any pair of intervals with uncertain boundaries. The "100% overlap probability" reported in `README.md` is an artifact of this metric rather than a finding about the archaeology.
- `stan/sequential_model.stan` fixes the temporal order of deposits to the arbitrary order of the site labels, so the hypothesis it encodes depends on how the data frame was sorted. It also places a sampling statement on a locally computed `gap` without a Jacobian.
- No existing program addresses multiple occupation episodes at a site, inbuilt age, outliers, or the count of contemporaneous occupations, all of which are required here.

## 11. Environment

The project library started empty; `renv.lock` recorded only `renv` itself. Setup is complete as of 2026-08-13, with CmdStan 2.38.0 installed. Three packaging problems were resolved and are recorded here so the environment can be rebuilt.

- `data.table` fails to load when built from source against Homebrew `libomp` on this machine. Installed from the CRAN macOS binary instead (1.18.2.1). `cmdstanr` depends on it.
- `sf` fails to build unless Homebrew's GDAL, GEOS and PROJ are ahead of Anaconda's on `PATH`, because `gdal-config` resolves to `/opt/anaconda3/bin` by default. `rcarbon` depends on `sf`. Setup must export `PATH=/opt/homebrew/opt/{gdal,geos,proj}/bin:$PATH`.
- `IntCal` is used for the calibration curve rather than `rcarbon`, because it supplies IntCal20 at 1-year spacing across the whole study range with no geospatial dependencies. `rcarbon` is retained solely as the independent calibration check of section 6.5, so a future rebuild that cannot compile `sf` loses only that check, not the analysis.
