# Bayesian Contemporaneity of Late Prehistoric Occupations in the Upper Susquehanna Valley

**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Status:** Design, approved for planning
**Data:** `data/Local Dates.xlsx` (167 radiocarbon determinations)

## 1. Research questions

1. How many settlements in this part of the Susquehanna valley were occupied at the same time, and how does that number change through the sequence?
2. How long was each occupation, and how much do occupations overlap?
3. How strongly do the data support contemporaneous occupation as against strictly serial occupation?

The analysis must answer these as posterior distributions with stated uncertainty, not as point estimates.

## 2. Data

### 2.1 Contents

The spreadsheet holds 167 determinations in a single sheet with columns `site`, `material`, `lab_no`, `c14_age`, `c14_error`, `reference`, and two unnamed note columns. Radiocarbon ages range from 200 to 1760 BP. Errors range from 20 to 210 years.

### 2.2 Site and context parsing

The `site` column concatenates site name and excavation context (`Bates_66`, `Chenango_Point_F102`, `Owego Southside Sewage Treatment_Sq 7/70`). Parsing on the context suffix yields 39 sites, of which 16 have four or more determinations and 10 have six or more.

The site is the analytical unit, because the research question concerns villages. Context is retained as a column so that inferred occupation phases can be checked against excavation units.

Parsing is not fully mechanical. `Herrick Hollow I`, `Herrick Hollow II`, and `Herrick Hollow V` are separate sites; `Chenango_Point`, `Chenango_Point_South`, and `Chenango_Shores_East` are separate sites; `Broome_Tech` and `Broome_Tech_PAF` may or may not be. The parse is therefore implemented as a **checked-in lookup table** (`data/site_parse.csv`) mapping every distinct `site` string to a site and a context, generated by a rule and then hand-audited. The table is the authority, not the rule.

### 2.3 Material classification

Two classes drive the old-wood model:

- **short_lived** (n = 83): maize, bean, squash, seed, nutshell, hazelnut shell, bitternut shell, butternut shell, marshelder. Mean error 40 years.
- **wood** (n = 84): wood, charred wood, wood charcoal, charcoal, "probably wood", "charred material", "carbonized material". Mean error 75 years, maximum 210.

"Charred material" and "carbonized material" are genuinely indeterminate. They are assigned to `wood` because that is the conservative assignment for inbuilt age, and the assignment is recorded as `uncertain = TRUE` so a sensitivity run can move them.

Classification is a checked-in lookup table (`data/material_classes.csv`) keyed on the verbatim material string, hand-audited, not a regex evaluated at runtime.

### 2.4 Data quality flags

The following are reported in a data-quality document and retained in the analysis. They are not silently corrected.

- Lab code `AA-41933` appears twice at Thomas_Luckey with different determinations (483 ± 58 at F140, 409 ± 45 at PM3345). One is probably a transcription error.
- Two Sidney Hangar determinations have `lab_no = "none provided"` and very different ages (1030 ± 50, 370 ± 80).
- `Beta-7007?` at Roberson carries a "Check" note.
- Four determinations carry notes that Prezzano 1992 reports a different error (± 200 rather than ± 100) or a different age. The spreadsheet values are used; the discrepancy is reported.
- The note "Hart excluded these in his study" attaches to the Funk 1993 and Prezzano 1992 wood determinations.

## 3. Model

### 3.1 Notation

Index determinations by `i = 1..N`, sites by `s = 1..S`. Determination `i` belongs to site `s(i)` and material class `c(i)`. Observed age `y_i`, error `sigma_i`.

Calendar time is in cal BP throughout, so larger values are older. A phase runs from `a` (start, older) to `b` (end, younger) with `a > b`.

### 3.2 Occupation phases

Site `s` has `J_s` candidate occupation phases:

- `J_s = 4` for sites with 6 or more determinations (10 sites: Bates, Broome_Tech_PAF, CCE_Site, Chenango_Point, Chenango_Point_South, Jamba, Owego Southside Sewage Treatment, Roundtop, Street, Thomas_Luckey)
- `J_s = 2` for sites with 3 to 5 determinations (12 sites)
- `J_s = 1` for sites with 1 or 2 determinations (17 sites)

This gives 81 candidate phases in total, of which the model is expected to leave many inactive. The 17 single-phase sites contribute occupation windows whose duration is driven almost entirely by the hierarchical prior of section 3.7, and every result is reported both including and excluding them.

Each phase has a midpoint `m_sj` and a duration `d_sj > 0`, with `a_sj = m_sj + d_sj/2` and `b_sj = m_sj - d_sj/2`. Within a site the midpoints are declared `ordered`, which resolves label switching. Determinations within a phase are uniformly distributed across it, which is the standard Bronk Ramsey phase model.

Phase membership is unknown. Site `s` carries a simplex `pi_s` over its phases, with

```
pi_s ~ Dirichlet(alpha / J_s)
```

and `alpha = 1`. This is an overfitted mixture in the sense of Rousseau and Mengersen (2011): because `alpha / J_s < 1`, and each component carries two parameters, components not supported by data are driven toward zero weight rather than splitting the data arbitrarily. The number of occupation episodes at a site is therefore inferred rather than fixed.

### 3.3 Marginalizing the latent calendar dates

Stan cannot sample discrete parameters, so phase membership must be marginalized. Marginalizing over phases requires the probability of a determination given a phase, integrated over the unknown calendar date. That integral is computed analytically from precomputed quantities rather than by introducing latent date parameters.

For each determination, the calibration likelihood on a calendar grid is

```
L_i(t) = Normal(y_i | mu(t), sqrt(sigma_i^2 + tau(t)^2))
```

where `mu` and `tau` are the IntCal20 mean and error. This is evaluated on a uniform grid spanning 0 to 2500 cal BP at 1-year spacing and treated as piecewise linear between knots. Its exact integral

```
Phi_i(t) = integral from t_min to t of L_i(u) du
```

is then piecewise quadratic, and is evaluated in Stan at arbitrary `t` by locating the cell (constant time, since the grid is uniform) and applying

```
Phi(t) = Phi_g + L_g * h + (L_{g+1} - L_g) * h^2 / (2 * Delta),   h = t - t_g
```

This is continuously differentiable in `t`, so the gradient is well behaved for Hamiltonian Monte Carlo.

The probability of determination `i` given phase `(a, b)`, with the calendar date integrated out under the uniform-phase assumption, is

```
A_i(a, b) = (Phi_i(a) - Phi_i(b)) / (a - b)
```

Three consequences follow. The `1/(a-b)` factor is applied once per determination, which is the correct uniform-phase normalization. All `N` latent calendar-date parameters are eliminated, which removes the funnel geometry and speeds sampling. The likelihood is smooth in the phase boundaries.

### 3.4 Old wood

A wood sample's growth date precedes the depositional event by an inbuilt age. Modeling the inbuilt age as exponential with mean `kappa`, the event-date likelihood is the convolution of `L_i` with that exponential. Convolution is a fixed linear operation on the grid, so it is precomputed rather than evaluated during sampling.

The convolved likelihood and its integral are precomputed at six values of `kappa`: 0, 15, 30, 60, 120, and 240 years. A simplex `q` over these six values is estimated, shared across all wood determinations. `kappa = 0` recovers the uncorrected likelihood, so the model can conclude that inbuilt age is negligible.

The inbuilt-age scale is identifiable because nine sites carry both wood and short-lived determinations, and the offset is learned from that contrast rather than assumed. Short-lived determinations are fixed at `kappa = 0`.

| Site | short-lived | wood |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bates | 4 | 10 |
| Chenango_Point | 7 | 5 |
| Roundtop | 6 | 5 |
| Thomas_Luckey | 18 | 2 |
| CCE_Site | 6 | 1 |
| Chenango_Point_South | 6 | 1 |
| Deposit_Airport1 | 2 | 2 |
| Herrick Hollow II | 2 | 1 |
| Lamb | 1 | 2 |

Bates, Chenango_Point, and Roundtop carry most of the information, since they are the only sites with five or more of each class. The estimated inbuilt age therefore rests on three sites, and figure 7 reports its posterior against its prior so that the weight of evidence behind it is visible. If the posterior tracks the prior, the correction is an assumption rather than an inference and will be described that way.

Prior: `q ~ Dirichlet(1)`.

### 3.5 Outliers

Every determination carries a Christen (1994) outlier component: with probability `rho_c` the determination is drawn from a likelihood computed with error inflated by a factor of 5. Outlier rates are estimated separately for wood and short-lived material, with `rho_c ~ Beta(2, 18)` (prior mean 0.10).

The inflated likelihood and its integral are precomputed alongside the others.

### 3.6 Full likelihood

For determination `i` at site `s`:

```
p(y_i) = sum over j of  pi_sj * [ (1 - rho_c) * sum over k of q^c_k * A_i^(kappa_k)(a_sj, b_sj)
                                + rho_c * A_i^(outlier)(a_sj, b_sj) ]
```

with `q^short = (1, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0)` fixed and `q^wood` estimated. Evaluated on the log scale with `log_sum_exp`.

### 3.7 Duration prior

Duration is the parameter most exposed to prior choice, because a site with two or three determinations cannot constrain its own occupation length. Durations are therefore given a hierarchical prior pooled across all phases at all sites:

```
d_sj ~ Lognormal(mu_d, sigma_d)
mu_d ~ Normal(log 60, 1)
sigma_d ~ HalfNormal(1)
```

Pooling means that sites with enough determinations to constrain duration inform sites that do not. The prior mean of 60 years is a weakly informative starting point consistent with published estimates of northern Iroquoian village occupation length.

Because this choice matters, the primary model is refit under `mu_d ~ Normal(log 25, 1)` and `mu_d ~ Normal(log 150, 1)`, and every headline quantity is reported under all three. If the number of contemporaneous villages moves materially across these, that is the finding and it will be stated as such.

Phase midpoints receive a uniform prior over the calendar grid, subject to the within-site ordering.

## 4. Derived quantities

Computed in Stan's `generated quantities` block, so uncertainty propagates correctly.

**Phase allocation.** For each posterior draw, the discrete allocation `z_i` is drawn from its full conditional. This recovers the discrete structure that was marginalized out of the likelihood.

**Phase activity.** Phase `(s, j)` is active in a draw if at least one determination is allocated to it. This is the definition of an occupation episode used throughout: an episode exists when there is dated evidence for it. It requires no weight threshold.

**Site occupancy.** `O_s(t) = 1` if any active phase at site `s` covers calendar year `t`.

**Contemporaneous village count.** `C(t) = sum over s of O_s(t)`, evaluated on a 10-year reporting grid. This is the primary result. Its posterior is summarized by median and 50% and 95% credible intervals at each year. Reported twice: over all 39 sites, and over the 22 sites with three or more determinations, since the singly and doubly dated sites contribute windows whose width comes from the prior rather than from their own data.

**Pairwise overlap.** For each site pair, the number of calendar years in which both are occupied, `V_ss' = integral of O_s(t) * O_s'(t) dt`. Reported as a posterior distribution, together with `P(V >= 25 years)`. The existing repository code reports `P(V > 0)`, which saturates at 1 for any pair of fuzzy boundaries and carries no information; it is replaced, not extended.

**Occupation duration.** Posterior duration for each active phase, and total occupied span per site.

**Active phase count.** Posterior number of active phases per site, which reports directly on whether a site is a single occupation or a palimpsest.

**Log likelihood.** Per determination, for model comparison.

## 5. Model comparison

Four models, compared by leave-one-out cross-validation.

| Model | Structure | Question it represents |
|---|---|---|
| `M-single` | One phase covering all determinations | The valley was occupied as a single undifferentiated episode |
| `M-indep` | One phase per site, unconstrained | Each site is a single occupation, overlap unconstrained |
| `M-multi` | Multiple phases per site, unconstrained | Primary model |
| `M-seq` | One phase per site, all phases constrained non-overlapping | Villages were occupied strictly in series |

`M-single`, `M-indep`, and `M-multi` are the same Stan program under different data configurations, differing only in the site grouping and in `J_s`. `M-seq` is a separate program using a global ordered boundary vector.

`M-seq` requires an ordering of sites, and the space of orderings is combinatorially large. Sites are ordered by their median calibrated date, which is the ordering most favourable to the sequential hypothesis. This is stated explicitly as a limitation. If the sequential model still loses under its best available ordering, the conclusion against strict serial occupation is correspondingly stronger; if it wins, the result is suggestive rather than decisive, because the ordering was chosen using the data.

Comparison uses PSIS-LOO with Pareto-k diagnostics reported. Mixture models with few observations per component frequently produce unreliable Pareto-k values. Where more than five percent of k values exceed 0.7, exact 10-fold cross-validation is run instead and reported in place of PSIS-LOO.

## 6. Validation

No result is reported before these pass.

1. **Prior predictive check.** Simulate from the prior and confirm the implied distributions of occupation duration, phase count, and contemporaneous village count are archaeologically plausible and not pathological.
2. **Simulation recovery.** Simulate 30 datasets with known phase structure, using the real site sample sizes, the real measurement errors, and the real calibration curve. Fit `M-multi` and check coverage of credible intervals for duration, phase count, and `C(t)`. This is the test of whether multi-phase structure is recoverable at three determinations per site. The expectation is that it partly is not, and the recovered coverage determines which sites can carry an interpretation.
3. **Sampler diagnostics.** R-hat below 1.01, bulk and tail ESS above 400, zero divergent transitions, E-BFMI above 0.3. Failures are fixed, not reported around.
4. **Posterior predictive checks.** Compare observed determinations against the posterior predictive distribution, by site and by material class.
5. **Numerical check on the calibration machinery.** Compare the precomputed `Phi` interpolation against direct numerical integration of `L` for a sample of determinations and phase windows, and confirm agreement to within tolerance. Compare single-date calibrated distributions against `rcarbon::calibrate` output.
6. **Prior sensitivity.** All headline quantities under the three duration priors of section 3.7.

## 9. Expected outcome

Much of this assemblage falls between roughly 700 and 300 cal BP, where IntCal20 has plateaus that limit temporal resolution regardless of model quality. The realistic expectation is a broad posterior on the village count, wide durations at thinly dated sites, and a limited number of site pairs for which non-contemporaneity can be demonstrated. That is a legitimate result and will be reported as such. Demonstrating that contemporaneity cannot be resolved at a given scale is not the same as demonstrating that many villages were occupied simultaneously, and the write-up will not blur the two.

## 10. Defects in the existing code, and why it is not reused

Recorded so the decision is auditable.

- `stan/contemporaneous_model.stan` applies the uniform-phase normalization `-log(theta_start - theta_end)` once, where the uniform-phase model requires it once per determination. Combined with Stan's automatic Jacobian for the declared bounds on `calendar_dates`, the net penalty on phase duration is wrong, and durations are biased.
- `stan/partial_overlap_model.stan` maps `calendar_dates_raw` to `calendar_dates` through `inv_logit` and uses the result in the likelihood without the corresponding Jacobian adjustment. It also places a standard normal on the raw parameter, which implies a logit-normal distribution of dates within a phase, peaked at the centre, rather than the uniform distribution the phase model requires. Both bias duration.
- The `overlap_prob` generated quantity in the same program is a 0/1 indicator of any overlap. Averaged over draws it gives `P(overlap > 0)`, which saturates at 1 for any pair of intervals with uncertain boundaries. The "100% overlap probability" reported in `README.md` is an artifact of this metric rather than a finding about the archaeology.
- `stan/sequential_model.stan` fixes the temporal order of deposits to the arbitrary order of the site labels, so the hypothesis it encodes depends on how the data frame was sorted. It also places a sampling statement on a locally computed `gap` without a Jacobian.
- No existing program addresses multiple occupation episodes at a site, inbuilt age, outliers, or the count of contemporaneous occupations, all of which are required here.
